## In one paragraph

KEPT is a private social investing habit app. You make one small weekly promise — *twenty
dollars, every Friday* — you keep it by buying a tokenized stock from your own wallet, and
a handful of people you trust see **that** you kept it, never **how much**. The app reminds
you before your day runs out, tells your friends the moment you follow through, and lets
them cheer you on or nudge you while your week is still open. Your streak is real: it's
backed by an actual purchase, not a checkbox you ticked. And nobody sees a balance, a
holding or a dollar figure unless you decide they should.

---

## The problem

Investing on a long horizon is lonely, abstract, and perfectly designed to be postponed.
Nothing bad happens on the Friday you skip. Every other habit worth building has a moment
where somebody notices you didn't do it. Investing has none.

The apps that exist make it worse in two ways.

**Portfolio apps put your balance at the top of the screen.** Over a week that number is
noise — it moved because the market moved, which you didn't do. So the app teaches you to
feel good about something you didn't cause and bad about something you couldn't prevent,
while the one thing you actually control — whether you put money in this week — appears
nowhere.

**Social investing apps solve loneliness by making performance public.** Leaderboards,
returns, copy trading. That produces exactly the behaviour it measures: more trading, more
churn, more risk. It also explains why most people share nothing financial with friends.
The fear isn't looking like a bad investor — it's people knowing *how much money you have*.
Asking someone to show friends their portfolio is asking them to show friends their net
worth. They decline, and they're right to.

So two people who genuinely want to build this habit together have nothing. They can send
screenshots, which exposes everything, or say nothing, which is what actually happens.

---

## What makes KEPT different

**Consistency is the score, not returns.** KEPT measures whether you showed up. No
leaderboard — a leaderboard in a wealth app is just a ranking of who started with more. The
only person you're ahead of is the version of you who didn't open it.

**Your friends see the ring, not the number.** This is the unlock. Strip the social layer
down to *progress only* and sharing becomes something people will actually do. Your circle
knows you kept your promise fourteen weeks running. They have no idea whether that promise
is ten dollars or two hundred. The accountability survives; the exposure doesn't.

**The promise is verified, not self-reported.** A habit app with a checkbox is a to-do
list — you can tick it on a bad week and nobody knows. Because KEPT settles through your
own Solana wallet, the app independently confirms the purchase happened before it fills
your ring or tells your circle anything.

And **nobody's money is pooled.** No group wallet, no shared account, no one holding anyone
else's funds. You can leave a circle and lose nothing, which is exactly why joining one
feels safe.

## The loop

1. **Promise.** Pick an amount and a day. *"$20, every Friday."*
2. **Circle.** Invite a few people you trust.
3. **Connect.** Link your Solana wallet once.
4. **Invest.** Choose an asset, see what your money buys, approve it in your wallet.
5. **Kept.** Your ring closes, your streak extends, your widget updates.
6. **Noticed.** Your circle sees you followed through — and reacts.

Everything else exists to make step 6 feel good enough that step 4 happens again.

## How KEPT keeps you coming back

A weekly habit lives or dies on what happens in the six days between actions.

### The rings

Three concentric arcs are the first thing you see every time you open the app.

- **Promise** (outer, kiwi green) — did you do what you said this week?
- **Goal** (middle, violet) — how close are you to your destination?
- **Circle** (inner, coral) — how many of your people are on track?

Three questions, three answers, one glance. An incomplete circle is visually unbearable in
a way an unticked checkbox is not. You don't read anything — you see a gap.

And the Promise ring **resets every week**. That's the engine. A ring that only fills is a
progress bar, and a progress bar at 68% creates no urgency. A ring that empties every
Monday gives you a fresh, small, completable task fifty-two times a year.

### A streak that forgives you

Keep your promise, extend your streak. Miss a week and it resets — which is where most
habit apps lose people, because the session after a broken streak is often the last one.

So KEPT gives you more than one thing to be proud of. Alongside the streak there are
**perfect months** — a finished calendar month with nothing missed. A streak, once broken,
is broken forever. A month can be perfect again starting the first. Miss a Friday in week
nine and you still have something in reach. That choice is aimed squarely at the week that
decides whether someone stays.

The missed week itself is never punished: a neutral, incomplete state. Never red, never an
alert, never a "you broke your streak" screen. Shame is a bad retention strategy and a
worse relationship with money.

### Twelve awards, none of them for being rich

| | |
|---|---|
| **Showing up** | First promise · First month · Steady eight · Quarter rhythm · Half a year · A full year |
| **Whole months** | Perfect month · Three perfect months |
| **Together** | Not alone · Small circle |
| **Encouragement** | First nudge · Good neighbour |

Nothing for portfolio size, returns, or number of trades. You cannot buy a single one.

And **half the collection is earned through other people** — having a friend in your
circle, sending ten nudges. An earlier version derived every award from the streak alone,
which rewards whoever was already disciplined and says nothing about the thing that makes
KEPT different. The social habit is what makes the financial one stick, so it's rewarded
like it.

The app always shows the **closest** award, not the next in the list. One nudge away beats
forty weeks away.

### It reminds you before you fail, not after

A reminder that arrives after you've missed is a notification about disappointment. As your
day approaches with your week still open, you get *"Your promise is within reach. A small
step this week keeps the rhythm alive."* It opens straight to the buy screen — acting on it
is two taps.

The tone is deliberate: warm, plain, never alarming. Not "YOU'RE ABOUT TO LOSE YOUR
14-WEEK STREAK." A product that makes you anxious about money gets deleted, and should. You
get one reminder per promise — the app won't badger you twice about the same week.

### Your friends find out the moment you follow through

The instant your purchase confirms, your kept promise posts to the circle feed and lands on
their screens live — your ring updates in their app while they're looking at it.

What they receive is your name and the fact that you kept it. No amount, no asset, no
balance.

Then they react — 👏 💜 🔥 🙌 — and you feel it. Four reactions, no comment box, tap twice
to take one back. There's deliberately no text: comments on financial activity invite
advice, and unsolicited investment advice from friends turns a supportive group into a
stressful one.

Keeping your promise stops being an admin task and becomes a small, visible, social moment.
And when someone in your circle keeps theirs, you see it — quietly the strongest reminder
in the product. Nothing makes you open your wallet like watching someone else do it first.

### Nudges

When someone's week is running out, you can **nudge** them: *"A nudge from your circle.
[Your name] is cheering you on. Your week is still open."*

Three rules stop it becoming a place people get poked: one nudge per person per week, only
into an open week, never at yourself. The button only appears on a friend who can actually
be nudged, so the app never offers a dead action.

Because sending them earns awards, the app explicitly rewards you for showing up *for other
people*. That's the behaviour that keeps a circle alive past week three.

### On your home screen

A native widget shows your rings, so progress is ambient — you see the gap while looking
for something else, which is when most habits are actually saved. It only ever holds
progress. A phone face-up on a table shows your consistency and nothing about your money.

### Sharing that doesn't expose you

> *"14 weeks kept in a row. Amount stays private. — KEPT"*

That's the whole message. It's shareable precisely *because* it's empty of money.

## What your circle can and cannot see

**They see:** your name · that you kept this week, or that your week is open · how far along
your goal ring is, as a shape · your streak · your awards.

**They never see:** how much you promised · how much you've invested · what you bought ·
what it's worth · whether you're up or down · your balance, holdings, or portfolio.

Not "hidden by default." Your friends' copy of the app has no way to ask for those numbers
about you. The only person who sees your money is you.

The default is **progress only** and you never have to change it. You can share more with a
circle you trust completely, but nothing pushes you toward it, and the product works
perfectly if nobody ever does.

There's a second thing this buys, which isn't obvious until you think about who uses it:
**it makes the habit shareable between people with very different amounts of money.** A
student putting in $10 a week and a friend putting in $500 have identical rings. They hold
each other accountable and compare streaks as equals, because the only number either can
see about the other is *weeks*. An app that showed amounts would make that friendship
impossible. This one makes it the normal case.

Circles are invite-only, invites expire, and there's no public feed or discovery. KEPT is
for people who already know each other.

## Real assets, real ownership

**You're buying tokenized stocks and ETFs** — Apple, Nvidia, the S&P 500, gold; around 930
assets through xStocks, priced live. A real position, not points or a balance the app holds
for you.

**It's your wallet, and only yours.** You approve each purchase in your own wallet app and
the asset lands in your wallet. KEPT never touches your funds and cannot move them. If KEPT
disappeared tomorrow your portfolio would be unaffected — it isn't in KEPT. That's why a
group habit product can exist without anyone trusting anyone with money.

**Twenty dollars works.** Fractional by default, so a small promise buys a real slice of an
expensive share. The product is aimed at the person who's been meaning to start for two
years.

**It runs on your schedule.** Your promise day is your promise day. A habit that can only
be kept during business hours isn't one most people can keep.

**And it's what makes the streak real.** Because the purchase is a confirmed transaction
against a wallet you've proven you own, the app verifies it before it counts. This is the
one thing in the product that genuinely couldn't be built another way, and everything
social rests on it.

### Private markets, through Tessera

Alongside public equities there's a small **private markets** lane powered by Tessera:
pre-IPO exposure to **OpenAI, SpaceX and Kalshi**. They sit near the top of the shelf —
they're among the most recognisable names in the catalogue — but each carries a **"Private"
chip** so they're never mistaken for the index funds beside them.

Each comes with a disclosure you actively accept before your wallet is involved. A Tessera
T-Token is a **loan participation right**, not company equity. You don't own a piece of
OpenAI; you have economic exposure tied to its valuation, repaid on a qualifying liquidity
event. No ownership, voting, dividend or information rights, and no fixed maturity.

Without that acknowledgement the purchase doesn't happen. A twenty-dollar-a-week habit app
has a particular obligation here — its user is explicitly not a sophisticated trader, and
putting an exotic instrument on the same shelf as an S&P 500 fund without saying loudly how
different it is would be indefensible.

### The buy screen tells you the truth

Before you spend, you see the current price and how it's moved over 5 minutes, an hour, 6
hours and 24 hours; exactly how much your amount buys at a live quote; what each unit
really costs once the trade is routed; and how deep the market is.

You type any amount you like — it isn't locked to your weekly promise, because a promise is
a habit, not a price list.

## Why it feels good to open

Most finance apps look like spreadsheets that lost a fight with a chart library. A habit
app you dread opening is one you stop opening.

**One thing on the screen, and it's beautiful.** The rings dominate. Colour travels *around*
the dial as a ring fills rather than sitting still; each ring's track is a dim version of
its own colour rather than grey, which makes the three read as one glowing object instead
of three progress bars; the leading edge casts a soft shadow onto the track behind it so
the ring looks physically real. They sweep into place with a gentle overshoot every time you
open the app. It's a genuinely satisfying half-second, and you get it every single time.

**Colour means something.** Green is your promise, violet your goal, coral your people.
Those three are never used decoratively anywhere else, so the moment you see coral you know
the app is talking about your friends. You learn the language without being taught it.

**It's warm, not corporate.** Soft cream background instead of banking blue-grey.
Generously round corners, soft ambient shadows instead of hard borders. It's the single
change that moves the whole thing from "fintech dashboard" to something you enjoy opening
on a Friday evening.

**Celebration where it's earned.** Awards arrive as glossy 3D objects — a seedling for your
first promise, fire for eight weeks, a trophy for a full year — and they're deliberately
rare, at most one per screen. Everything that's a moment feels like one; everything else
gets out of the way.

**Numbers are achievements, not data.** Your streak is set like a scoreboard, not a bank
statement.

**Nothing shouts.** A missed week is a quiet gap. No red alerts, no guilt screens. The app
is calm about money and playful about the habit, and never the other way round.

Every part of it serves one behaviour: closing a ring.

## Who it's for

**People who've been meaning to start for two years.** They know they should invest. They
haven't, because nothing ever made the first twenty dollars feel urgent.

**Friend pairs, couples, siblings, small trusted groups.** Two to five people close enough
to hold you accountable, not close enough that you'd show them your bank balance — which is
exactly the group the privacy model is built for.

**Small, consistent investors, not traders.** Anyone whose plan is "a bit, regularly,
forever" who keeps failing at the *regularly*.

**Solana Mobile owners.** Wallet already on the device, a home screen that carries a real

## Built for Solana Mobile

Your wallet is already on the phone, so connecting is one tap and buying is two — approve
in your own wallet app and come straight back. No bank transfer, no funding delay, no
settlement wait between deciding to keep your promise and having kept it. The gap between
intention and action is where weekly habits die; on this hardware it's about fifteen
seconds.

The home-screen widget is a real native widget drawing the app's own rings. Circle activity
arrives live, so a friend's kept promise appears while you're looking at the screen. And
verification happens in the background — you never wait on it, you just find the ring
already full.

## Why this one

**It's honest about money.** A habit app that celebrates balance size is a performance app
in disguise. KEPT never celebrates how much you have, charts no returns by default, uses a
neutral state for a missed week, and puts your money on one screen only you can see. Twelve
awards, not one of them buyable. The most private number in your life stays private — from
your friends and from the app's own social layer — and that's what makes sharing the rest
possible.

**It's honest about the chain.** No token, no yield, no pool, nothing on-chain for the sake
of it. Solana does two things nothing else does: it lets you own your portfolio outright in
your own wallet, so a group habit needs no group custody — and it turns "I invested this
week" from something you claim into something the app can check. Strip Solana out and KEPT
is a to-do list with friends.

Most investing apps ask what you're worth. This one asks whether you showed up.

**Promises compound.**
